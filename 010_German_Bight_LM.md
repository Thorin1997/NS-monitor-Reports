## 1. Introduction

Subsea infrastructure is a critical component of maritime energy and communications networks. Monitoring vessel activity in its vicinity can help identify spatial concentrations of close-proximity events and support a better understanding of maritime traffic patterns.

NS-Monitor examines AIS-derived vessel activity around power cables, pipelines, and telecommunications cables in the German Bight.

However, due to a programming error involving the database polygon and the infrastructure files, pipelines are not represented in the two charts presented in this briefing. The visualised results cover power cables and telecommunications cables only.

---

## 2. Methodology

The analysis uses AIS data received exclusively from AISStream. Recorded proximity events within 500 metres of monitored infrastructure segments were counted across multiple observation windows.

---

## 3. Results

During 1–4 October, bshcontiscables.8 recorded 10 events, followed by pcablesrijks.36 with 8

Across the longer 1–18 October period, bshcontiscables.8 recorded 11 events, while pcablesrijks.36 recorded 20.

These counts highlight differences in recorded proximity events across the monitored segments.

---

## 4. Limitations

AIS-based monitoring depends on transmission availability, data quality, and reception coverage. 

AIS observations therefore represent the vessel's reported position and activity and may not provide a complete picture of its movements or operational purpose.

The identification of repeated movements in proximity to subsea infrastructure does not, in itself, indicate suspicious or malicious activity. Additional contextual information, including a longer observation period and further vessel-specific data, would be required for a more comprehensive assessment.

---

## 5. Conclusion

NS-Monitor provides an AIS-based approach to monitoring vessel proximity around subsea infrastructure in the German Bight. These preliminary findings provide a basis for further longitudinal analysis.
