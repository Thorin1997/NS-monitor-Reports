## 1. Introduction

NS-Monitor has been adapted to support longitudinal monitoring of individual vessels operating in areas of interest. Rather than assessing broader maritime traffic patterns, this monitoring approach focuses on identifying and documenting repeated vessel activity over a defined period, particularly where movements occur in proximity to critical maritime infrastructure.
This report focuses on MMSI Bahamas, which Kpler identifies as an Other | Utility Vessel. The vessel was monitored on four separate occasions between 01 and 02 October 2026 within the German Bight, allowing assessment of its activity and movement pattern over a short longitudinal observation period.

---

## 2. Methodology

During the monitoring period of 01–02 October 2026, NS-Monitor was used to identify and review activity associated with MMSI Bahamas within the German Bight.
The assessment focused on the vessel's repeated presence in the monitored area, its reported speed, movement pattern, and geographic proximity to subsea infrastructure. The vessel was monitored on four separate occasions during the two-day observation period.
Across all four monitoring events, the vessel was observed travelling at a reported speed of below 1 knot. Each observation was assessed in relation to the surrounding maritime environment and the presence of relevant infrastructure.

---

## 3. Results

MMSI Bahamas was monitored on four separate daily observation windows over 01–02 October 2026 in the German Bight.
During all four observations, the vessel was recorded travelling at less than 1 knot, indicating a consistently low-speed movement pattern throughout the monitoring period.

The vessel was observed operating in proximity to the following infrastructure:

rijkscables.24         | vessel distance: 454m | 2026-10-02 10:52:23

bshcontiscables.8 | vessel distance: 136m | 2026-10-02 17:10:29

bshcontiscables.8 | vessel distance: 124m | 2026-10-02 19:50:01

The combination of repeated observations within the same general area, low reported speed, and proximity to infrastructure makes the vessel relevant for continued longitudinal monitoring.

However, the observed pattern alone does not establish the operational purpose of the vessel or indicate suspicious or malicious activity. Further observations over a longer period would provide additional context for assessing whether the activity represents a recurring operational pattern.

---

## 4. Limitations

AIS-based monitoring depends on transmission availability, data quality, and reception coverage. AIS observations therefore represent the vessel's reported position and activity and may not provide a complete picture of its movements or operational purpose.
The observation period was limited to two days, during which the vessel was monitored on four occasions. This relatively short timeframe limits the ability to determine whether the observed low-speed activity represents a persistent operational pattern or a temporary condition.
The identification of repeated movements in proximity to subsea infrastructure does not, in itself, indicate suspicious or malicious activity. Additional contextual information, including a longer observation period and further vessel-specific data, would be required for a more comprehensive assessment.
Vessel identification and vessel-type information may vary between AIS-derived sources and commercial maritime databases. In this assessment, the vessel type is based on the classification provided by Kpler.

---

## 5. Conclusion

MMSI Bahamas, identified by Kpler as an Other | Utility Vessel, was monitored on four separate occasions between 01 and 02 October 2026 within the German Bight.
The repeated low-speed activity observed over the two days provides a basis for continued longitudinal monitoring of the vessel. At present, the available observations document a recurring presence and low-speed movement pattern but do not, in isolation, establish suspicious or malicious activity.
Further monitoring would help determine whether the observed behaviour is part of a consistent operational pattern and provide additional context for assessing the vessel's activity in relation to the surrounding infrastructure.
